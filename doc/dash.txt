Dash reimplementation
=====================

Step 1: input only
------------------

The initial step added two bytes: first-tap direction and a countdown. Previous button
state belongs to src/pad.s (buttons_prev). init_move resets the tap state
through init_dash. The gesture is read once per movement update.

read_dash:

* Finds new presses with current buttons AND NOT previous buttons.

* Saves a first directional press and starts a 12-update countdown.

* Returns +1 (Right) or -1 (Left) for a matching second press before expiry.

* Returns zero otherwise. Holding a direction never counts as another press.

* An opposite press starts a new pair; both directions together cancel the
  pair.

* A completed pair is consumed. A and B do not affect detection.

Initially update_move ignored the return value. read_dash itself has no
ground/air rules; those belong to dash eligibility, not the gesture.

Build with make, then run python3 tools/test_dash_input.py (requires py65).

Step 2: separate velocity rules from position proposals
-----------------------------------------------------

update_move now calls set_velocity_x and set_velocity_y before update_x and
update_y. The velocity routines contain the existing walking, jump and gravity
rules. The position routines only compute next = position + velocity; they do
not read buttons or apply acceleration. Collision and commit are unchanged.

This was a behavior-preserving split to let dash select different velocity rules
while sharing the position and collision code.

Step 3: basic air burst
-----------------------

The initial burst implementation started only when the previous update
reported no supporting floor contact (hit_y=0). It stores a signed dash_velocity
and a dash_timer, separate from the tap-window timer. DASH_SPEED=8 pixels/update;
DASH_FRAMES=8 updates, including the second-tap update.

During the burst, update_dash sets vel_x to the fixed burst velocity, clears
vel_y and returns A=1. update_move skips the normal velocity routines, clears
acc_y and sets jump_latch so zero vertical velocity cannot enable a midair jump.
update_x, update_y, collision resolution, position commit and camera remain shared.

Initially stop_dash_on_contact cleared the timer after wall or floor/slope contact.
Input cannot steer or extend the burst, and taps during it are not queued.
After the last burst update, walking/gravity resume normally on the next update.
A remains jump-only. At this step ground double-taps still walked. Another
double-tap in the same airtime is allowed; the charge limit is a later step.

No substeps were added: collision uses the normal old/proposed positions.
Tests cover wall crossings in both directions, a one-sided exit, and uphill
slope contact; existing mixed-contact limitations and horizontal byte-wrap
behavior are unchanged. Spikes and subpixel X are not included.

Run python3 tools/test_dash.py for burst tests after rebuilding.

Step 4: surface bursts and slope launches
----------------------------------------

A double-tap now starts a burst from support too. The new dash_mode byte
distinguishes an air-started burst, a surface burst, and a surface-to-air launch.
Surface floor contact carries the burst instead of cancelling it. A fresh A
press while supported interrupts it for an ordinary jump; jumping also wins
over a simultaneous second direction tap.

Collision reports hit_y_slope for the selected supporting surface: 0 for flat,
-1 for 1:1 slopes, -2 for 2:1 slopes. Surface velocity is the signed horizontal
speed multiplied by this gradient. Thus a rightward 1:1 dash has velocity
(+8, -8), and a rightward 2:1 dash has (+8, -16). This keeps horizontal speed
constant, not total speed along the diagonal.

Resting collision now also recognizes an upward trajectory exactly following
a rising slope's resting height. Proposals above that height still detach;
upward subpixel movement on flat ground still detaches as before.

When a surface burst loses support, its current velocity is retained for the
remaining burst updates. This includes launches on the first burst update and
flat ledge exits (zero initial vertical velocity). Once the timer expires,
update_dash returns DASH_COAST: keep horizontal velocity, but run ordinary
vertical rules so gravity resumes. Landing or wall contact ends the coast;
directional input alone does not steer it. A fresh airborne double-tap starts
a horizontal burst instead, clearing the launch's vertical velocity.

update_x and update_y remain unchanged. finish_dash_move replaces the old
stop_dash_on_contact routine to distinguish carrying support from landing.
inc/dash.inc documents the three velocity-selection results for update_move.

Coordinate limits have not changed: X/Y still have 8-bit whole-pixel positions.
Fast slope launches can reach the top of the screen and expose the existing
Y-wrap behavior; no ceiling or scrolling-world policy is added in this step.
Mixed floor/wall and endpoint-overlap collision limitations also remain.

Run python3 tools/test_surface_dash.py for slope/launch regression tests.

Camera catch-up
----------------

The existing screen-X tracking frame remains 96..160. Outside that frame,
update_camera now scrolls 1 + floor(distance outside / 4) pixels per update,
instead of always one pixel. Near the frame, walking/catch-up remains slow;
farther away it speeds up enough to track dash velocity. When the ball slows
or stops, decreasing distance slows the camera until it reaches the frame.
There is no extra camera state or dash-specific camera path. Camera byte-wrap
still toggles ntable in either direction. Tests: tools/test_camera.py.

Next review steps, each kept separate:
1. One dash per airtime and landing recharge.
2. Review boundary behavior and any further high-speed collision cases.
