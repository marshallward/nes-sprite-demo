First air-dash prototype
=======================

Surface double-tap extension
----------------------------

On floor/slope contact or in the air, tap Left or Right, release it, then press the same
direction within DASH_TAP_WINDOW (12 updates). Holding or alternating
directions does not start it; opposing simultaneous directions clear the pair.
Tap pairs can span leaving a ledge or landing. They are forgotten during a burst
or after consuming the air charge, so no chained burst is queued.
B remains reserved for future attack.

Surface dash uses the same DASH_PIXELS/DASH_FRAMES settings as air dash. Floor
contact carries rather than cancels this mode, including uphill/downhill following.
Walls still stop it; hazards are checked on each pixel. Leaving the surface
converts to the remaining gravity-free air burst, consumes the airborne charge
and latches jump. A fresh A press while still supported cancels the surface burst
and runs normal jumping, starting a new airtime. Air dash is still available after
that legitimate jump. A has priority over a simultaneous second direction tap.

Ground support does not recharge during an active burst. It recharges on the
next ordinary supported update, so holding a direction cannot auto-chain dashes.
Four extra ordinary-RAM bytes: surface-mode flag, previous direction bits, first
tap direction, tap countdown. Nine dash bytes total; zero-page use is unchanged.
Horizontal movement now uses 8.8 X; dash substeps retain the fractional byte
and move the whole-pixel byte. Normal walking is .75px/update, while dash speed
is unchanged. Existing dash suites use an explicit 1px walking fixture;
tools/test_subpixel_x.py tests double-tap/dash at the shipped .75px speed too.

python tools/test_surface_dash.py covers both directions, tap
window/expiry, holding/opposing taps, air double-tap, jumping/cancel priority,
walls/spikes, slope following and edge conversion without a free jump. Headless
Mesen verified double-tap -> ground -> 1:1 uphill -> air continuation (X20 ->85,
including the first normal step). Sampled surface burst updates cost 10183 flat,
10517 uphill, 12026 downhill cycles on this map, excluding controller/main-loop/
NMI/DMA. These are representative samples, not worst-case bounds (~34..40% NTSC).
The earlier air-dash section below describes air mode, not surface-mode contact.

Input/behavior
--------------

Double-tap Left OR Right in air (not both), just like on the ground.
A only jumps; releasing/repressing it never triggers a dash. B is unassigned.
Directional double-taps work whether A is held or released.
The burst travels in that direction at 8 pixels/update for 8 updates (64 pixels total in
open air), regardless of subsequent steering/button release. Vertical velocity
is cleared; gravity and vertical integration are skipped throughout the burst.
Fractional Y is held as well. Normal movement/gravity resumes the next update.

One dash per airtime shared across directions, recharged on floor contact. Eligibility uses the previous
update's hit_y, not vertical velocity (which is also near zero at the apex).
A supported jump press takes priority over a simultaneous direction tap and
clears the pending pair. Airborne A presses do not affect tap detection.
Wall contact cancels the dash without recharging it. Floor/slope contact applies
normal floor response and ends/recharges the burst. Those contacts may adjust Y;
the dash does not ignore supporting geometry to maintain its height.
Starting a dash also latches jump, including when entering the air by walking
off rather than jumping. This prevents the dash's zero vertical velocity from
allowing a midair jump after the burst. Landing/release rearms normally.

Implementation
--------------

* src/dash.s: init_dash, update_dash; inc/dash.inc contains speed, duration and
  return codes. Five air-dash state bytes plus four surface-tap/mode bytes.
  No new zero-page allocations. Existing movement variables hold each proposal.
* update_dash returns A=0 when ordinary movement should run, A=1 when it already
  handled movement/camera, A=2 when it touched a hazard. src/game.s selects that
  path before ordinary update_move and uses the existing death handler for hits.
* Each dash update makes up to DASH_PIXELS one-pixel horizontal proposals and calls
  existing apply_move_collision per pixel. Y integration is not repeated.
  check_hazards runs after EVERY committed substep, stopping at first touch.
  Camera follows once per game update, not four times. This is not teleporting
  and does not require a general continuous collision solver.
* init_dash runs on game initialization, death and respawn. Respawn also clears
  stale floor contact because the safe spawn is in the air. Dash-specific X wrap
  cancels the burst at 0/255; ordinary movement's broader boundary policy remains
  deferred. Existing one-sided walls are honored; known corner-shape and mixed
  contact limitations still apply. The rejected rescan remains disabled.

Verification / cost
-------------------

python tools/test_dash.py tests the built ROM. Coverage:
duration/distance/vertical hold, double-tap timing and direction, held-A input,
apex, recharge behavior, wall/one-sided-wall/slope response, hazard path,
safe travel above spikes, camera rate, boundary stop, and reset on respawn.
Leftward tests mirror distance/vertical hold, locked direction, wall/one-sided-wall
blocking, spike contact, shared recharge, camera rate and stopping at X=0.
Additional input tests cover double-taps while holding jump, ignored A re-presses
and B presses, and supported jump priority. Gameplay jump tests
establish floor contact before the initial A press, as during ordinary play.
The walk-off/dash regression checks both holding A and releasing/repressing A
after the burst: neither may create another jump before landing. Distance tests
read DASH_PIXELS/DASH_FRAMES from the built symbols rather than assuming speed4.

Headless Mesen also verified jumping, a 32-pixel dash over spikes at Y=171,
then resumed falling. HISTORICAL timing at 4 pixels/update: 3947..4796 cycles
(13..16.1% of an NTSC frame), excluding controller/main-loop/NMI/DMA overhead.
These samples do not describe the current 8-pixel speed and are NOT a worst-case
bound. Up to eight scans now run per update; remeasure before using the old
percentage as a budget. Flat movement and slope
following rules themselves were not changed.
